Privacy Policy

PicKeeper: Photo Cleaner

Effective date: July 21, 2026

Short version: Your photos are analyzed entirely on your iPhone and are never uploaded to us or anyone else. PicKeeper has no user accounts and no photo servers. The only data that leaves your device is anonymous app-usage statistics and, if you view an event, approximate coordinates sent to Apple for a place name.

1. Who We Are

This policy explains how PicKeeper: Photo Cleaner ("PicKeeper", "the App", "we", "us", or "our") handles information. It applies to the PicKeeper iOS app distributed on the Apple App Store.

Contact: feedback@globalpixelstudio.com

2. Your Photos Never Leave Your Device

PicKeeper requests access to your photo library so it can analyze your photos on your device. This analysis includes detecting duplicate and similar images, scoring image quality to pick the best shot in a burst, reading text inside screenshots, and matching photos to a phrase you type in search.

All of this runs locally on your iPhone. We do not upload, transmit, collect, view, store, sell, or share your photos, videos, thumbnails, the text recognized inside your screenshots, or the search phrases you type. We operate no server that receives photo data, and we have no technical ability to access your photo library.

The machine-learning models used for analysis are bundled inside the App or provided by iOS itself. PicKeeper works with the network turned off; only the optional place-name lookup and analytics described below require connectivity.

3. Information We Actually Collect

Category What it is Why
Anonymous usage & diagnostics Which screens are opened, whether a scan completed, counts such as "a cleanup batch of N items was staged", subscription funnel steps (paywall shown, purchase started, purchase result, restore tapped), app version, device model, OS version, coarse country, and crash information. To understand which features are used, fix crashes, and improve the App.
Purchase status Whether an active subscription or lifetime purchase exists, as reported by Apple's StoreKit. To unlock paid features. Handled by Apple; we never see your payment method.
Approximate event coordinates When PicKeeper groups photos into an event, it may send that event's approximate latitude/longitude to Apple's geocoding service to obtain a place name. To label an event like "Weekend · Sha Tin". No image is sent. Results are cached on device. If you are offline, events display without a place name.

We never attach photo content, file names, album names, recognized screenshot text, or search queries to any analytics event.

4. What We Do Not Collect

  • No photos, videos, or thumbnails.
  • No text extracted from your screenshots.
  • No search phrases you type into PicKeeper.
  • No user accounts, email addresses, passwords, phone numbers, or contacts.
  • No precise location tracking, and no background location access.
  • No advertising identifier (IDFA). PicKeeper does not show ads and does not run an ad network.
  • No selling or renting of personal information to anyone, ever.

5. Service Providers

PicKeeper uses a small number of third-party services, none of which receive your photos:

  • Google Firebase Analytics — anonymous app-usage and crash statistics. We use the build that excludes advertising-identifier support. See Firebase privacy information.
  • Umeng (Youmeng, part of Alibaba) — aggregate app-usage statistics. See Umeng privacy policy.
  • Apple — App Store distribution, in-app purchases, on-device machine-learning frameworks, and the optional geocoding lookup described above. See Apple's privacy policy.

These providers process data under their own privacy policies. We do not authorize them to receive photo content, and PicKeeper does not send them any.

6. Data Stored on Your Device

PicKeeper keeps an index of your library on the device — for each photo, things like a compact fingerprint, a quality score, whether it is a screenshot, any text recognized inside it, and its date and coordinates. It also keeps a Recycle Bin list of items you staged for deletion. This index exists only on your iPhone, is covered by iOS device encryption, and is never transmitted.

Deleting PicKeeper removes this index and the Recycle Bin list from your device. Photos that were already permanently deleted through iOS cannot be restored by reinstalling the App.

7. Data Retention

On-device index data persists until you delete the App or clear it in the App. Anonymous analytics are retained by our analytics providers according to their standard retention windows, typically no longer than 14 months for event-level data.

8. Children

PicKeeper is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child has provided us information, contact us and we will address it.

9. Your Rights

Depending on where you live — including under the EU/UK GDPR, the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA/CPRA), and similar laws — you may have rights to access, correct, delete, port, or restrict processing of personal information, and to object to processing or withdraw consent.

Because PicKeeper has no accounts and collects no directly identifying information, we usually hold nothing that identifies you personally. You can nevertheless:

  • Revoke photo access at any time in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Photos.
  • Limit analytics by turning off "Share iPhone Analytics" and "Personalized Ads" in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security.
  • Delete the App to remove all on-device data.
  • Email feedback@globalpixelstudio.com to make a request. We do not sell or share personal information for cross-context behavioral advertising.

10. International Transfers

Our analytics providers may process anonymous usage data on servers outside your country, including in the United States and mainland China (Umeng). Where required, such transfers rely on appropriate safeguards offered by those providers.

11. Security

Because photo analysis never leaves your device, the main protection for your photos is your device itself. We rely on iOS sandboxing and device encryption. Keep your device updated and protected with a passcode or Face ID. No method of transmission or storage is completely secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security of the limited data we do collect.
